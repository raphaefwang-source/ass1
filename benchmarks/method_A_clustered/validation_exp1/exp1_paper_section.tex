\subsection{Experiment 1: accuracy of the periodic Ewald--PPPM operator}\label{sec:exp1}

All tests use the screened longitudinal kernel $K(\mathbf r)=c\,e^{-\kappa r}r^{-1}\hat{\mathbf r}\hat{\mathbf r}^{\mathsf T}$
with $\kappa=0.7$ and density $\rho=0.75$. The operator is the full periodic lattice sum with the $k=0$ Fourier term
retained, split at $s=\xi^2$ into $K=K_S+K_L$.
The reference $\Gamma_{\rm ref}$ is the lattice-summed operator: near images are summed directly, far images by a
Chebyshev fit, with relative tolerance $10^{-13}$. On all 21 configurations it agrees with an independent, unsplit
direct image sum to radius $55$ to within $2.0$--$4.2\times10^{-15}$ in relative spectral norm. For $N=512$ it agrees
with a stricter lattice sum (tolerance $10^{-15}$) to $2.7\times10^{-15}$, and on every test vector the two references
differ by at most $1.3\times10^{-14}$ relative to $\|\Gamma_{\rm ref}X\|_2$.
We use uniform, moderately clustered and strongly clustered configurations with periodic minimum separation $0.9$
(\cref{fig:exp1}(a)): three independent seeds per family at $N=128$ and $256$, and one configuration per family at
$N=512$, the strongly clustered one being the configuration on which the production parameters were optimised.

\paragraph{Splitting identity}
The three radial factors are evaluated independently: $\theta$ analytically, $\theta_S$ in closed form, and $\theta_L$
by its own $s$-quadrature, so $K_L$ is never formed as $K-K_S$.
We take all pair images with $|\mathbf d|\le8$ of the nine $N=256$ configurations and $\xi\in[0.5,1.5]$. The relative
Frobenius error of $K-K_S-K_L$ is at most $3.0\times10^{-16}$, and the largest single-pair error is
$1.9\times10^{-15}$ (\cref{fig:exp1}(b)).
A radial sweep down to $r=10^{-6}$ stays below $1.5\times10^{-15}$, and all three factors agree with 50-digit values to
$1.0\times10^{-15}$ relative to $\theta$. By contrast, the subtraction $\theta-\theta_S$ would lose up to
$9\times10^{-3}$ of $\theta_L$ at $r=10^{-4}$.

\paragraph{Short-range cutoff}
With $\xi=0.85$, the long-range operator is replaced by its mesh-free Fourier lattice sum $\Gamma_L^{\rm exact}$
(certified tail $\le1.7\times10^{-17}$), so
$E_S=\|\Gamma_S^{r_c}+\Gamma_L^{\rm exact}-\Gamma_{\rm ref}\|_2/\|\Gamma_{\rm ref}\|_2$ contains no mesh error.
For $s=\xi r_c\in[2,6.6]$, least-squares fits $\log E_S=a-cs^2$ on $s\in[2.0,5.3]$ give $c=0.981$--$0.987$ for the
nine configurations. We report $c$ as an observed decay constant, not a universal exponent. The certified real-space
bound exceeds the observed error by a factor $1.4$--$1.9$ (\cref{fig:exp1}(c)).
For $s\ge5.8$ the exact truncation tail falls below the measured error, which stays at
$3.5$--$4.1\times10^{-15}$. This value is the reference/round-off floor, and it also confirms the splitting identity
at the operator level. These points are shown as open markers.

\paragraph{Matrix-free action}
The quantity used by the solver is the action $\Gamma_hX$, so we measure
\[
E(X;M)=\frac{\|(\Gamma_h(M)-\Gamma_{\rm ref})X\|_2}{\|\Gamma_{\rm ref}X\|_2}
\]
for the production matrix-free implementation. For each configuration, $\xi$, $r_c$, $k_c$ and $p=9$ are fixed and
only the FFT size $M$ varies. Grids that violate the Nyquist condition are reported as invalid; on every valid grid, all
reciprocal shells with $|\mathbf k|\le k_c$ are retained.
The same unit test vectors are used for every $M$: ten Gaussian vectors, the three slowest and the fastest eigenvectors
of $\Gamma_{\rm ref}$ (our choice of spectral probes), and the three translations.
To expose the mesh convergence we use strict cutoffs, $r_c=6/\xi$ and $k_c\le12\xi$. Their truncation error is at most
$5.1\times10^{-15}$, so the remaining error comes from the mesh.
For all three configuration families and $N=128$, $256$ and $512$, the Gaussian-probe error decreases from about
$10^{-9}$ to $1$--$1.4\times10^{-14}$ (\cref{fig:exp1}(d)). The fitted rates are $10.1$--$10.3$ for the Gaussian and
$10.0$--$10.3$ for the slow eigenvectors, and the families differ by at most $12\%$ at equal $N$ and $M$.
At the production parameters ($r_c=4.8878$; $k_c=9.3585$, the exact shell $2\pi\sqrt{172}/L$ for $N=512$; $p=9$), the
error does not depend on $M$ on any admissible grid: it equals the truncation error of the fixed cutoffs. Over all 21
configurations this is $2.0$--$2.9\times10^{-8}$ for the Gaussian vectors, $4.9$--$8.1\times10^{-8}$ for the slow and
$0.84$--$2.0\times10^{-8}$ for the fast eigenvectors.
The slow eigenvectors have the largest relative error because their reference action $\lambda_{\min}\|u\|$ is the
smallest; their operator-normalised error is not larger.
The translation residual $\|\Gamma_hT\|_2/\|\Gamma_{\rm ref}\|_2$ stays below $3\times10^{-16}$, below that of
$\Gamma_{\rm ref}$ itself. Finally, $\Gamma_h$ is symmetric to $1.4\times10^{-17}$ and positive on the zero-momentum
subspace for every grid.
