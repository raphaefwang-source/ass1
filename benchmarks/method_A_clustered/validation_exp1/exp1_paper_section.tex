\subsection{Spatial accuracy of the periodic Ewald--PPPM operator}\label{sec:exp1-spatial}

All tests use the screened longitudinal kernel $K(r)=c\,e^{-\kappa r}r^{-1}\hat r\hat r^{\mathsf T}$ with
$\kappa=0.7$, density $\rho=0.75$, the full periodic lattice sum with the $k=0$ Fourier term retained, and the
Gaussian-scale split $K=K_S+K_L$ at $s=\xi^2$. The reference $\Gamma_{\rm ref}$ is the lattice-summed operator (direct near images,
Chebyshev-fitted far images, relative tolerance $10^{-13}$); on all 19 configurations it agrees with an independent, unsplit direct image sum to radius
$55$ to within $2.1$--$4.2\times10^{-15}$ in relative spectral norm, which sets the reference floor. Uniform,
moderately clustered and strongly clustered configurations with $d_{\min}=0.9$ are used, three independent seeds
per family.

\paragraph{Split identity}
The three radial factors are evaluated independently: $\theta$ analytically, $\theta_S$ by its closed form, and
$\theta_L$ by its own $s$-quadrature, so $K_L$ is never formed as $K-K_S$. Over all pair images $|d|\le8$ of the
nine $N=256$ configurations and $\xi\in[0.5,1.5]$, the relative Frobenius error of $K-K_S-K_L$ is at most
$3.0\times10^{-16}$ and the largest single-pair error is $1.9\times10^{-15}$
(\cref{fig:exp1}(a)). A radial sweep down to $r=10^{-6}$ stays below $1.5\times10^{-15}$, and all three factors
agree with 50-digit values to $\le1.0\times10^{-15}$ relative to $\theta$. By contrast, the subtraction
$\theta-\theta_S$ loses up to $9\times10^{-3}$ of $\theta_L$ at $r=10^{-4}$, which is why the long-range part is
never evaluated in real space by subtraction.

\paragraph{Short-range cutoff}
With $\xi=0.85$ the long-range operator is replaced by its mesh-free Fourier lattice sum (certified tail
$\le1.7\times10^{-17}$), so the error $E_S=\|\Gamma_S^{r_c}+\Gamma_L^{\rm ex}-\Gamma_{\rm ref}\|_2/
\|\Gamma_{\rm ref}\|_2$ contains no mesh contribution. For $s=\xi r_c\in[2,6.6]$, least-squares fits
$\log E_S=a-cs^2$ on $s\in[2.0,5.3]$ give $c=0.981$--$0.987$ for the nine configurations; we report $c$ as an
observed decay constant, not a universal exponent. The certified real-space bound exceeds the observed error by a
factor $1.4$--$1.9$. Beyond $s\approx5.8$ the error reaches $3.5$--$4.1\times10^{-15}$
($1.8$--$2.4\times10^{-15}$ against the direct sum), which confirms the split identity at the operator level.
No cutoff step left the neighbour set unchanged, and all points are shown.

\paragraph{Degree convergence}
The discrete degree $D_i^h=\sum_{j\ne i}K^h_{ij}$ is the production FFT degree with the mesh self response
$W_{ii}$ removed; it matches the diagonal blocks of the assembled $\Gamma_h$ to $5.7\times10^{-16}$. Omitting the
self-response correction would produce an apparent error of up to $1.6\times10^{-3}$. At fixed $(\xi,r_c,k_c)$ only
the grid size $M$ is varied, and every point satisfies $m_c<M/2$ and $\eta<1$; Nyquist-violating grids are reported
as invalid. The mesh-transfer error against the exact truncated Fourier degree converges with fitted rates
$4.1$, $6.4$, $8.8$ and $10.2$--$10.4$ for $p=4,6,8,9$, uniformly in $N\in\{128,256,512\}$ and in the configuration
family. With the production cutoffs ($r_c=4.8878$, $k_c\le9.3585$), the complete error levels off at the
fixed-cutoff floor $2.0$--$2.7\times10^{-8}$ (\cref{fig:exp1}(c)). For $p=9$ every admissible grid already lies
on this floor, so the plateau reflects truncation rather than a lack of mesh convergence.

\paragraph{Matrix-free action}
Finally, the production matrix-free action is compared with $\Gamma_{\rm ref}X$ for $N=128$ and $256$ on
Gaussian, spectral (extremal eigenvectors), slow-mode and translation probes. Translation probes are judged by
absolute residuals, which are exactly zero for the pair-difference form and $\le9.2\times10^{-18}$ for the cached
form. With the production cutoffs the dense error is $E_{\rm op}\le3.5\times10^{-8}$ for all $M$ and all 18
configurations, below the budget $2\times10^{-7}$. With stricter cutoffs ($r_c=6/\xi$, $k_c\le12\xi$), the
Gaussian-probe error decays at a fitted rate $M^{-10.2}$ to $10^{-14}$ (\cref{fig:exp1}(d)). Throughout,
$\Gamma_h$ is symmetric to $2\times10^{-17}$ and positive on the zero-momentum subspace, and its smallest eigenvalue
there deviates from that of $\Gamma_{\rm ref}$ by at most $7.8\times10^{-8}$ (relative).
