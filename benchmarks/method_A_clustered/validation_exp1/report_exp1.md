# Experiment 1 (Model A): accuracy validation of the periodic Ewald–PPPM friction operator

Experiment 1 now has one set of results and makes three claims:

1. The Ewald splitting identity K = K_S + K_L holds in double precision (Fig. 1(b)).
2. The short-range truncation error decays as a Gaussian in s = ξ r_c, down to the reference/round-off floor
   (Fig. 1(c)).
3. The matrix-free PPPM action Γ_h X is accurate for every tested class of input vectors. With ξ, r_c, k_c and p fixed,
   it converges under FFT mesh refinement; the mesh-only error is separated from the complete error (Fig. 1(d)).

Fig. 1(a) shows the particle configurations used. The diffusion-coefficient (degree D_i) study of the first version
is no longer part of Experiment 1 (Section 7, "Changes").

Every number below is read from `results/`. The tables are in `results/tables_exp1.md`, generated by
`report_tables_exp1.py`. Figures:
- Figure 1: `figures/fig1_spatial_validation.{pdf,png}`.
- Supplementary: `figures/figS1_split_radial`, `figS2_action_detail`, `figS3_translation_residual`, `figS4_action_p`.

## 1. Model, operator and references (fixed)

**Model:**
- Kernel A: K(r) = g(r) r̂r̂ᵀ with g(r) = c e^{−κr}/r, κ = 0.7, c = γ r_ref e^{κ r_ref} = 1.6148 (γ = 0.5,
  r_ref = 1.3), density ρ = 0.75.
- Operator: (ΓX)_i = Σ_{j≠i} Σ_n K(q_i − q_j + nL)(X_i − X_j).
  - Full periodic lattice sum, with the Fourier k = 0 term retained.
  - No minimum image, no free-space or regularised kernel.
- Ewald split at s = ξ²:
  - K_S = c r rᵀ ∫_{ξ²}^∞ ρ_κ(s) e^{−sr²} ds
  - K_L = c r rᵀ ∫_0^{ξ²} ρ_κ(s) e^{−sr²} ds
- **Production action** Γ_h X = Γ_S^{r_c} X + D_L^h X − H_L^h(X):
  - the pair-difference `GammaH.matvec` (the measured-fastest implementation at N = 512);
  - a second path, the cached D − H form `CachedGammaH.apply`.
- Neither the kernel, the split, the PPPM implementation nor the Lanczos code was changed.

**References:**

| reference | construction | use |
|---|---|---|
| Γ_ref | `prl_toy_models.LatticeFriction`, `rel_tol = 1e-13`: near images direct, far images by a tensor Chebyshev fit, k = 0 included | (c), (d) |
| Γ_D55 | unsplit direct periodic image sum to R = 55 (`direct_periodic.DirectPairs`); certified tail bound 5.3–6.4e-14 relative | accuracy of Γ_ref |
| Γ_ref′ (N = 512) | `LatticeFriction`, `rel_tol = 1e-15`, Chebyshev degree 20, `near_cells = 3` | convergence of Γ_ref |
| θ, θ_S, θ_L at 50 digits | mpmath quadrature of the two s-integrals | (b) |
| Γ_L^ex | mesh-free Fourier lattice sum to k = 2ξ·6.6 = 11.23; certified tail ≤ 1.7e-17 relative | (c) |
| Γ_L^{k_c} | the same Fourier sum over exactly the modes the PPPM retains, with no mesh assignment | mesh-only error in (d) |

**Measured reference accuracy:**
- ‖Γ_D55 − Γ_ref‖₂/‖Γ_ref‖₂ = **2.1–4.2e-15** over all 19 configurations.
- For N = 512: ‖Γ_ref′ − Γ_ref‖₂/‖Γ_ref‖₂ = **2.7e-15**.
- At the action level, every non-null probe gives ‖(Γ_D55 − Γ_ref)X‖/‖Γ_ref X‖ ≤ **1.0e-14** (`E_D55_vs_ref`) and
  ‖(Γ_ref′ − Γ_ref)X‖/‖Γ_ref X‖ ≤ 1.3e-14 (N = 512).
- Errors near 1e-14 are therefore at the reference/round-off level, and are flagged as such.

## 3. Panel (b): analytical Ewald splitting (results reused, not recomputed)

**Design** (`run_exp1.py split`, results `split_*`):
- θ, θ_S and θ_L are evaluated independently: analytically, by the erfcx closed form, and by θ_L's own graded
  Gauss–Legendre s-quadrature. K_L is never formed as K − K_S.
- Tensors are formed for every pair image with |d| ≤ 8 of the nine N = 256 configurations (204–207 thousand images
  each), for ξ ∈ {0.5, 0.6, 0.75, 0.85, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5}.
- A radial sweep r = 1e-6 … 12 covers small r.
- 25 radii per ξ are compared with 50-digit mpmath values.

**Results:**
- Relative Frobenius error of K − K_S − K_L: **2.0–3.0e-16**. Largest single pair: **1.9e-15**. Largest absolute
  entry: 8.9e-16 (max |K| = 0.94).
- Radial sweep down to r = 1e-6: ≤ 1.5e-15.
- All components agree with mpmath to ≤ 1.0e-15 relative to θ.
- Forming K_L as K − K_S instead would lose up to 9.3e-3 of θ_L at r = 1e-4 (Fig. S1). The independent quadrature stays
  at ≤ 6.7e-16.
- **Conclusion 1:** the identity holds at float64 round-off for every ξ and family. That is a few u (u = 1.1e-16), not
  ≤ 1e-15 for every single pair.

## 4. Panel (c): short-range cutoff error (results reused; plot revised)

**Design** (`run_exp1.py shortrange`, results `shortrange*`):
- ξ = 0.85, s = ξ r_c = 2.0, 2.1, …, 6.6, on the nine N = 256 configurations:
  E_S = ‖Γ_S^{r_c} + Γ_L^ex − Γ_ref‖₂/‖Γ_ref‖₂, with a mesh-free Γ_L^ex.
- Also recorded: the exact-arithmetic tail ‖Γ_S^{full} − Γ_S^{r_c}‖₂ and the certified bound ε_S.

**Results:**
- E_S falls from 1.3–1.4e-2 (s = 2) to the floor at s ≈ 5.8.
- Least squares of log E_S = a − c s² on s ∈ [2.0, 5.3] gives **c = 0.981–0.987** (9 configurations); s ≥ 3 alone
  gives 0.983–0.988. c is an observed decay constant, not forced to 1.
- The certified bound is 1.40–1.88 × the measured error.
- At production s = 0.85 · 4.88775618844242 = 4.155: E_S(4.1) = 4.7–5.6e-8 and E_S(4.2) = 2.1–2.4e-8.
- **Floor:** 3.5–4.1e-15 against Γ_ref and 1.8–2.4e-15 against Γ_D55. Γ_S^{r_c} + Γ_L^ex reproduces the full periodic
  operator to this level, which confirms the split identity at the operator level as well.

**Floor presentation (the horizontal segment in the first version):**
- The raw data were checked: for s ≥ 5.8, E_S is constant at 3.5–4.1e-15, while the exact-arithmetic truncation tail
  keeps falling (5.6e-17 at s = 6.6).
- The flat segment is therefore the reference/round-off floor, not an algorithmic error.
- A point is classed as resolved when its exact tail is ≥ 2 × the configuration's measured floor. That holds for
  s ≤ 5.7 in all nine configurations; s = 5.8–6.6 (9 points per configuration) are at the floor.
- Resolved points are drawn as continuous median curves with min–max shading. Floor points are drawn as open markers
  (every seed, unaltered), over a grey band labelled "reference / roundoff floor".
- The e^{−s²} guide covers only the resolved interval. The certified bound is labelled "theory" and drawn only down to
  1e-16.
- No value was removed or changed.

## 5. Panel (d): matrix-free PPPM action convergence (recomputed)

### 5.1 Design (`run_exp1.py action`, results `action_*`)

**Configurations and cutoffs:**
- N = 128 and 256: 3 families × 3 seeds each. N = 512: strongly clustered `strong_N512_s1`.
- For each fixed (configuration, cutoff setting, p = 9), only the FFT size M changes:
  - N = 128: M = 16–64; N = 256: 20–80; N = 512: 26–96. The Nyquist-invalid boundary candidates are included and
    recorded.
- **Production cutoffs:** the measured N = 512 optimum in full double precision (`results_psearch/optimum_strong_N512_s1_main.json`):
  - ξ = 0.85, r_c = 4.88775618844242, k_c = 9.358534792617089 = 2π√172/L₅₁₂, p = 9; production M = 40.
  - At N = 128 and 256 the same physical cutoffs are used; k_c snaps to the largest shell ≤ k_c of that box (n = 68 and
    n = 108). These cutoffs were not re-optimised for those N.
- **Strict cutoffs** (convergence experiment only): r_c = 6/ξ and k_c ≤ 12ξ, so the mesh error can be followed below the
  production truncation floor.
  - Shells: n = 81, 128 and 204 for N = 128, 256 and 512.

**Mesh validity, checked at every M:**
- Nyquist m_c < M/2 and η = k_c h/π < 1.
- The retained mode count equals an independent integer count of the modes with |m|² ≤ n_c, so every shell is complete.
- No mode falls below the influence-function threshold |Ŵ|² ≤ 1e-10.
- **Invalid points:** 57 of the 418 (configuration, cutoffs, M) cases fail Nyquist. They are kept in
  `action_mesh.csv` with the reason:
  - production: M = 16, 20, 26 for N = 128, 256, 512;
  - strict: M = 16/18, 20/22, 26/28.
- Valid points: 0 excluded modes everywhere; min |Ŵ|² = 7.1e-4.

**Probes:** fixed per configuration, unit 2-norm. The identical bytes are reused for every M, checked by SHA-256 at
each M.
- 10 Gaussian X ~ N(0, I_{3N}): seeds 1001–1010 (`numpy.random.default_rng(seed)`), translation component removed.
- Spectral:
  - eigendecomposition of sym(Γ_ref);
  - the three translation eigenvalues (|λ| ≤ 1.1e-15) are identified by their overlap with the translation space
    (1 to 3e-15);
  - slow probes are the eigenvectors of the 3 smallest positive eigenvalues; the fast probe is that of the largest
    eigenvalue.
  - These are our own choices; the Landau paper does not specify its spectral probes.
- 3 translations 1_N ⊗ e_a/√N. For these, only absolute and operator-normalised residuals are reported, never a ratio
  to ‖Γ_ref T‖ ≈ 0.
- Synthetic Maxwellian velocity vectors are not used. With T₀ = m = 1 and normalisation, they have the same
  distribution as the Gaussian probes.

**Errors:**
- E_complete(X; M) = ‖(Γ_h(M) − Γ_ref)X‖₂/‖Γ_ref X‖₂, using the production action.
- E_mesh(X; M) = ‖(Γ_L^h(M) − Γ_L^{k_c})X‖₂/‖Γ_ref X‖₂. Γ_L^h X is the mesh part of the production action
  (`Mesh.graph_action`); Γ_L^{k_c} is the exact truncated Fourier operator.
- E_trunc(X) = ‖(Γ_S^{r_c} + Γ_L^{k_c} − Γ_ref)X‖₂/‖Γ_ref X‖₂. It does not depend on M, and
  E_complete ≤ E_mesh + E_trunc held in every row.
- **For context only:** dense E_op and E_op,mesh, the symmetry of Γ_h, and λ_min on Range Π.
- **Fig. 1(d) y-axis:** every curve is the maximum over the tested probes, and over the configurations where several
  are pooled. It is not a mathematical maximum over all X.

### 5.2 Results, N = 512 strongly clustered (Table "(d) N = 512" in `results/tables_exp1.md`)

**Production cutoffs:**

| | Gaussian (10) | slow spectral (3) | fast spectral |
|---|---|---|---|
| E_complete, M = 28 | 2.03–2.20e-8 | 8.06e-8 | 9.35e-9 |
| E_complete, M = 30 … 96 | 2.01–2.14e-8 | 7.87–8.06e-8 | 8.43–8.85e-9 |
| E_trunc (M-independent) | 2.01–2.14e-8 | 7.87–8.05e-8 | 8.61e-9 |
| **E_complete at production M = 40** | **2.01–2.14e-8** | **8.05e-8** | **8.60e-9** |
| E_mesh, M = 28 → 40 → 96 | 3.7e-9 → 7.3e-11 → 1.4e-14 | 5.1e-9 → 8.7e-11 → 1.9e-14 | 2.8e-9 → 5.7e-11 → 1.2e-14 |

- **At M = 40:**
  - Dense E_op = 3.0344442e-8. The joint optimisation reported 3.0344442e-8; they agree to 1.1e-9 relative.
  - E_op,mesh = 1.9e-10.
- **Mesh-only error:** converges with least-squares rate **10.42** (Gaussian, M = 28–64, R² = 0.9995), 10.34 (slow) and
  10.40 (fast).
- **Complete error:** is the M-independent fixed-cutoff floor E_trunc for every valid M (fitted slope ≈ 0, R² ≤ 0.41).
  - The mesh part is visible only on the smallest grids: Gaussian +3% at M = 28; fast spectral 9.35e-9 at M = 28 and
    8.43–8.85e-9 at M = 30–32 (floor 8.61e-9). From M = 36 on, every complete error is within 0.5% of E_trunc.
  - So, at the production cutoffs, the accuracy is set by r_c and k_c, not by M.
  - This is an error floor of the fixed cutoffs, not non-convergence.
  - Against the 2e-7 budget: the dense E_op (3.03e-8) is 6.6× below it. The action errors are 9.3× (Gaussian) and
    2.5× (slow spectral) below it.

**Strict cutoffs:**
- E_complete equals E_mesh to 2–3 digits (E_trunc = 2.5e-15 Gaussian, 1.3e-14 slow).
- It falls from 1.6e-9 (Gaussian) / 2.0e-9 (slow) at M = 30 to **1.4e-14 / 2.2e-14 at M = 96**.
- Least-squares rate: 10.32 (Gaussian) and 10.15 (slow), on the points ≥ 1e-13 (M = 30–64). The last points sit at the
  reference/round-off level.

### 5.3 Results, N = 128 and 256 (three families × three seeds)

**Production cutoffs:**
- Gaussian E_complete ≤ 2.87e-8 (N = 128) and ≤ 2.76e-8 (N = 256), flat in M apart from the smallest admissible M.
- Slow spectral ≤ 6.74e-8 and ≤ 8.01e-8; fast ≤ 1.98e-8 and ≤ 2.00e-8.
- E_mesh falls to about 1e-14 at the largest M (6–9e-15).

**Strict cutoffs:** complete error from 1.0e-9 (M = 20) / 1.6e-9 (M = 24) to 1.0e-14 at the largest M.

**Fitted rates** (strict complete error and mesh-only error under either cutoff setting, Gaussian, slow and fast;
max over 10 probes × 9 configurations): **10.0–10.44**, with R² ≥ 0.998.

### 5.4 Which X is hardest?

**Relative complete error:** the slow spectral probes are hardest.
- At N = 512, M = 40: 8.05e-8 vs 2.14e-8 (Gaussian), a factor 3.8.
- Their reference action is the smallest: ‖Γ_ref u‖ = λ_min = 2.30, vs about 11.7 for Gaussian probes and
  ‖Γ_ref‖ = 17.55.
- The absolute error does not grow: the operator-normalised error is 1.07e-8 (slow) vs 1.42e-8 (Gaussian).

**Mesh-only error:** the slow modes are only 1.2–1.4× above the Gaussian probes. Mesh refinement treats all probe
classes alike: the rates agree to within ±0.2.

### 5.5 Translation null space and structural checks

**Translation residual ‖Γ_h T_a‖/‖Γ_ref‖₂** (Fig. S3):
- pair-difference: ≤ 1.5e-16; exactly 0 at N = 256, where 1/√N = 2⁻⁴ is exact;
- cached D − H: ≤ 2.9e-16;
- Γ_ref itself: 3.2–4.9e-16.

**Other checks**, over all 361 valid cases:
- The dense Γ_h agrees with the matrix-free action to ≤ 3.8e-15.
- Symmetry defect ≤ 1.3e-17.
- Cached vs pair-difference ≤ 2.2e-15.
- No negative eigenvalue on Range Π.
- λ_min(Γ_h) deviates from λ_min(Γ_ref) by ≤ 7.8e-8 (production) and ≤ 5.7e-11 (strict), relative.

**Spectral probes at N = 512:**
- Eigen-residuals ≤ 7.7e-15 relative.
- Relative gaps to neighbouring eigenvalues: 0.011, 0.011 and 0.017 (slow) and 0.004 (fast). The probes are
  well-defined eigenvectors, and their reference actions are far from zero, so relative errors are stable.

## 6. What the data support

**Directly measured:**
1. The split identity holds to ≤ 3.0e-16 (Frobenius) and ≤ 1.9e-15 (per pair), for ξ ∈ [0.5, 1.5], three families,
   down to r = 1e-6.
2. E_S decays as e^{−c s²} with c = 0.981–0.988 down to a 3.5–4.1e-15 reference/round-off floor. The certified bound is
   1.4–1.9× the observed error.
3. At the production parameters, the matrix-free action is accurate to 2.0–2.9e-8 (Gaussian), 4.9–8.1e-8 (slow
   spectral) and 0.8–2.0e-8 (fast spectral). The complete error is flat in M at the fixed-cutoff floor.
4. The mesh-only error converges with fitted rate about 10.3 for p = 9 at every N, family and probe class.
5. Under strict cutoffs, the complete action error converges to 1e-14.

**Interpretation, not proven here:**
- The cause of c < 1 (the algebraic prefactor of the tail).
- The cause of the odd-p rate p + 1.3.
- Behaviour for N > 512.
